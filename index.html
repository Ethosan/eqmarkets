    Methodology

    The needle is a Risk Appetite Index in the practitioner tradition of the Credit Suisse and Goldman Sachs risk appetite indicators (academic root: Kumar and Persaud, IMF, 2002). Each input is standardized against roughly its last 200 daily closes, z = (x - μ) / σ, then combined: risk-on inputs (ES, BTC, USD/JPY) enter positively and stress inputs (VIX, high-yield OAS, DXY) enter negatively. RAI is the weighted mean of those z-scores, shown in sigma units.

    Credit is measured with the ICE BofA US High Yield option-adjusted spread (FRED series BAMLH0A0HYM2), the standard series in the credit literature (Gilchrist and Zakrajsek, AER, 2012), rather than an ETF price. The cross-asset ρ chip is the average absolute pairwise correlation of daily log returns across ES, BTC, DXY, USD/JPY, and gold over about two months; readings above 0.55 indicate a macro-driven risk-on/risk-off regime in the RORO sense. Yen positioning comes from the CFTC Commitments of Traders report (Legacy Futures Only, non-commercial net contracts in JPY futures, weekly Tuesday data released Fridays). The carry-to-vol ratio divides the Fed-BOJ differential by 21-day realized USD/JPY volatility, a stated free proxy for the implied vol used in practice. The remaining chips show same-day moves for immediacy and do not drive the needle when z-history is available.

    Index weights are the loadings of the first principal component of the standardized input panel (~150 overlapping days), recomputed live each refresh, with an equal-weight fallback when history is insufficient. Positioning and the carry-to-vol ratio are context cards, not RAI inputs, to keep the index strictly daily. This is decision support and context, not a mechanical trading signal.
